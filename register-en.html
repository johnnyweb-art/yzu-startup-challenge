    How to enter

    Three steps, all online

    This competition follows the paperless policy, and registration is fully digital. A typical team prints nothing, signs nothing and submits no physical document, and can finish on a phone. We do not accept paper, email, walk-in or phone registration. There is no entry fee.

    PAPERLESS

    Three things are handled entirely online, with no supporting documents to submit: proof of enrolment is established by signing in to the form with your university account, so YZU students do not submit an enrolment certificate or student ID copy; consent is given by one tick box per member inside the form, with the account and submission timestamp recorded, which carries the same effect as a signature, so there is nothing to sign or scan; and the supervisor is confirmed by the Center directly by email, so no supervisor consent form is needed.

    Language: write your proposal in either Chinese or English. One language is enough, no bilingual version is needed, and your choice of language does not affect scoring. Teams may also pitch in English at the final round; note this in the remarks field of the registration form.

    Form a team and pick a track

    3 to 6 people with one team leader. The leader must be an enrolled YZU student. Teams may mix departments, colleges and year levels. Students from other universities may join, as long as YZU students make up at least half the team. One person should enter one team only.

    Prepare your proposal and upload it

    Proposals are uploaded as PDF, and Venture Track teams add evidence of traction. Consent is given by tick box inside the form, one per member, with nothing to print or sign. Only teams with a member under 18 or a member from another university upload an extra document, and the team leader does that.

    Submit, then wait for the Center's confirmation

    Submitting the form sends an automatic copy to your own address. That means received, pending review; it does not mean you are registered. After the eligibility check the Center sends a separate registration confirmation email, copied to every team member, and you are registered when that arrives. If the automatic copy does not reach you, email us before the deadline. The form closes automatically at 17:00 on 16 Oct 2026.

    FAQ

    What people ask before they register

    Can I enter on my own?
    No. A team is 3 to 6 people; finding people to build with is part of the exercise. If you have not found teammates yet, come to the kick-off briefing on 16 Sep or to either workshop. Other people there are looking too.
    Can first-year students enter?
    Yes, and we particularly encourage it. The Idea Track is designed for ideas that have not taken shape yet. If half or more of your members are first-year students admitted in AY 115 (2026), including first-year transfer students, tick Freshman Team when you register and you qualify for the guaranteed final slots and the Freshman Award.
    I have no startup experience and no product. Can I still enter?
    Yes. The Idea Track asks for a one-page proposal. No prototype, no revenue and no company required. Judges look at whether the problem you observed is real and whether the solution holds up, not at how far you have already got.
    Do we need a supervisor?
    No. One or two supervisors are optional, and having none affects neither your eligibility nor your score. A named supervisor may not also serve as a judge for this competition.
    We registered for the Idea Track. Can we switch to the Venture Track?
    Not after registration closes. Before the deadline you can edit your own form entry. If the eligibility check finds a clear mismatch, we will email the team leader to adjust it before the supplementary submission deadline.
    Can we team up with students from other universities?
    Yes. YZU students must make up at least half the team, and the team leader must be an enrolled YZU student. Members from other universities need to attach proof of enrolment from their own institution. Prize money is always paid to the team leader, who is a YZU student, and the team agrees its own internal split.
    Can we enter with a capstone project or a lab result?
    Yes, and this is where most Venture Track entries come from. Push what you have already built one step further: towards a business model, and towards something you can submit to FITI and U-start. If lab technology is involved, confirm IP ownership with your supervising professor first.
    Do I have to write in Chinese?
    No. Write your proposal in either Chinese or English. One language is enough, no bilingual version is needed, and your language choice does not affect scoring. You may also pitch in English at the final round; note this in the remarks field of the registration form.
    I am an international student. What else should I know?
    You are very welcome, and the Idea Track is a good place to start. Two things worth knowing. Language: your proposal may be in Chinese or English, one language only, and the choice does not affect scoring; you may also pitch in English at the final round by noting it in the remarks field of the registration form. Documents: the registration form is opened through a university account, and that sign-in record is what replaces a proof of enrolment, so students whose YZU account gives access to the form do not submit one. If your account status differs for any reason, email the Center before the deadline and we will confirm how to proceed.
    Can the same idea be entered by two teams?
    One person should enter one team only. If the same person is listed on two or more teams, we will email each team leader to settle on one entry before the supplementary submission deadline. If nobody replies in time, the team whose form was submitted earlier is the one kept.
    How long should the proposal be?
    One page for the Idea Track. Five to eight pages is a good length for the Venture Track. Both follow the proposal template, which carries a writing prompt in every section, so you can simply work through it.
    Are the workshops compulsory?
    No. Both workshops are open, free and take no attendance, and you can come before you register. 23 Sep covers market validation and business model design with GenAI; 7 Oct covers producing your proposal and pitch with GenAI. The final pitch is five minutes, and the second workshop practises exactly that.
    Is there an entry fee?
    No. Entry is free.
    Is it really zero paper?
    Yes. This competition follows the paperless policy and registration is fully digital. A team made up entirely of YZU students who are all 18 or over has nothing to print and nothing to sign, and can register on a phone. Only two situations call for one extra upload by the team leader: a team member under 18 (a single-page parental or guardian consent form with a signature), or a member from another university (their proof of enrolment).
    If nobody signs the consent form, what counts as consent?
    The full text of the consent terms is available to read inside the registration form, and every team member has their own compulsory tick box, which the team leader ticks after confirming with that member one by one. At the end of the form there is a team leader declaration. The system automatically records the account, the email address and the submission timestamp, and that record is the evidence of consent. The confirmation email is also copied to every team member, so you will know you have been listed and can reply to withdraw before the supplementary submission deadline if you object.
    Why don't YZU students submit proof of enrolment?
    Because the registration form has to be opened with a university account before you can fill it in and upload files. Being able to sign in already proves you are enrolled, so handing in a student ID copy on top of that adds nothing. Members from other universities have no YZU account, so their proof of enrolment is collected and uploaded by the team leader.
    Is everything online after we win too?
    No. Registration is fully paperless, but paying out prize money is not. The receipt, the internal split declaration, ID copies and bank account details still have to be submitted as paper originals, because the university accounting and cashier offices do not accept electronic signatures. This happens after the final in December and is handled by the team leader of each winning team, so leave time for it.
    How is the prize money split?
    Prize money is paid to the team representative, that is the team leader, against a signed receipt. How the team splits it internally is agreed within the team and recorded in a split declaration. The Center does not intervene and does not arbitrate.
    Can I call you?
    This competition is run digitally end to end, and enquiries are handled by email or online form only. We do not take enquiries by phone. Email usually gets a reply within one working day, and it leaves a record, so nothing falls through the cracks.
